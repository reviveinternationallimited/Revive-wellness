Muscle Is Medicine: Why Building Muscle Is the Secret to Ageing Well

By Marvin Fok

In the world of modern bodybuilding, people tend to picture big muscles with limited mobility - all show and no function.

Many question the purpose and importance of building muscle, aka hypertrophy. Especially in Asia, where the social standard of beauty favors a slim, slender look.

But as mankind lives longer and modern society faces population aging, longevity is directly linked to how much muscle you have and how well you can preserve it.

Muscles are not just dumb objects clinging to bones. Muscle is a health marker. It reflects how well your body metabolizes energy, how well it responds to insulin and regulates blood sugar, and even how it supports cognitive function.

Our movements depend on muscles lengthening, shortening, and contracting isometrically. Our muscles respond to force and stress from gravity. How we use our muscles determines the outcome. For example, prolonged sitting causes muscle atrophy, while resistance training drives muscle growth. In short: use it or lose it.

When we move under specific loads, our muscles experience different types of growth. One fascinating aspect of our physiology is what happens in the lengthened position under load. During a stretch under load, our body experiences longitudinal growth - in simple terms, the muscle fibers grow longer near the tendons, close to the joint attachments.

In our next post, we'll talk about how longitudinal growth protects our tendons and why most bodybuilding research is actually based on rehab.

This article is general information only and is not a substitute for individual medical advice. If you have symptoms or a health condition, please speak to your doctor or physiotherapist.
